Able250

The Campaign

Celebrating 250 Years of Independence for All

As America marks its 250th anniversary on July 4, 2026, Able250 ensures that people with disabilities are at the center of this historic celebration. For 250 years, Americans with disabilities have contributed to, fought for, and helped shape this nation — yet their stories are too often left untold.

Able250 is a campaign by Able Angel to honor that legacy and champion a future where interdependence, inclusion, and accessibility are cornerstones of American life. Through advocacy, storytelling, and community engagement, we are making sure the next chapter of the American story includes everyone.

Our Declaration

Declaration of Interdependence

Drafted by Able250© on behalf of all People with Disabilities on the 250th Anniversary of American Independence, July 4, 2026.

We the people with disabilities united in boundless spirit, vibrant diversity and shared humanity, rise on this historic occasion — the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence — to proclaim our Declaration of Interdependence. In the radiant light of that revolutionary document, which boldly affirmed the inalienable rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness, we weave our voices into the ever-evolving American tapestry. With hearts ablaze and hope unyielding, we declare our unwavering commitment to a future forged in mutual reliance, boundless inclusion, and the transformative power of unity.

Whereas, we celebrate that true independence is not a solitary endeavor but a symphony of interconnected souls, strengthened by community, sustained by compassionate systems, and elevated by the shared dreams of our fellow citizens. From the dawn of our nation, independence has been rooted in the sacred bonds of interdependence.

Whereas, for 250 years, people with disabilities have faced systemic barriers — enduring the shadow of discrimination, exclusion, and dehumanization. The eugenics movement branded us "unfit," while the Supreme Court's decision in Buck v. Bell sanctioned the forced sterilization of tens of thousands, stripping away bodily autonomy.

Whereas, we have overcome the segregation of children with disabilities from public education under the false guise of "separate but equal," the institutionalization of hundreds of thousands in abusive asylums and state schools, and employment barriers that relegated many to subminimum-wage workshops. Architectural, attitudinal, and policy obstacles have long denied us access to transportation, housing, healthcare, and the full embrace of civic life.

Whereas, our disabilities — whether visible or invisible, physical, sensory, cognitive, or intellectual — are threads in the rich fabric of our identities. They shape our perspectives but never define our worth, limit our potential, or diminish our sacred right to belong fully in society.

Whereas, our journey toward equality shines with triumphs — from the landmark passage of the Americans with Disabilities Act to the tireless march toward a more perfect union, where accessibility, dignity, and opportunity illuminate the path forward.

Whereas, interdependence is the heartbeat of progress, where every individual, regardless of ability, contributes to and is uplifted by the collective strength of our nation.

Now, therefore, we, the People with Disabilities, rejoice and proclaim with resolute conviction:

Our Commitment to Community

With open hearts, we pledge to nurture solidarity among ourselves and with all Americans, knowing our strength blooms in collaboration. We honor the caregivers, advocates, and allies whose unwavering support amplifies our voices and who rightly share in our triumphs and struggles.

Our Pursuit of Justice

We summon the courage to demand the enforcement and evolution of laws that safeguard our rights, dismantle disparities, and champion economic opportunity and social equality. We envision a nation where disability is not a barrier but a reflection of our distinctiveness celebrated as a vital part of our shared story.

Our Right to Equality

We claim our inalienable right to fully participate in the vibrant mosaic of American life — education, employment, healthcare, transportation, technology, and public spaces — free from barriers and the sting of discrimination. We call for universal design and policies that ensure every soul is seen, valued, and included.

Our Vision for Progress

We dream boldly of an America where interdependence is the cornerstone of democracy — a nation where responsibility, innovation, and empathy converge to create boundless opportunities, and where every person's unique contributions are cherished as a spark of collective greatness.

Our Legacy for Tomorrow

As we honor 250 years of American independence, we dedicate ourselves to building a future where people with disabilities are empowered to dream without limits, create with passion, and thrive in a society that uplifts every soul. Together, we forge a legacy of hope, unity, and unstoppable progress.

In witness whereof, we, the People with Disabilities, through Able 250, raise our voices in this Declaration of Interdependence, calling upon our fellow Americans to join us in this sacred mission. Let us unite, hand in hand, heart to heart, to forge a beacon of hope for the world. This is our shared promise as we celebrate and boldly create the next chapter of the American story.

Proclaimed with unwavering resolve this 4th day of July, 2026,
by ABLE250©, on behalf of America's People with Disabilities

The eugenics movement peaked in the early 20th century, promoting sterilization and segregation of those deemed "unfit." Buck v. Bell (1927) legalized forced sterilization; over 60,000 Americans were sterilized under such laws. Segregation in education persisted until landmark cases like PARC v. Pennsylvania (1971) and Mills v. Board of Education (1972). Institutions like Willowbrook State School and Pennhurst State School were exposed for widespread abuse in the 1970s. Sub-minimum wages under Section 14(c) of the Fair Labor Standards Act remain legal today, though increasingly challenged. Thirty-one states still have involuntary sterilization legislation in 2025.
